Abstract

The Space-Time Membrane (STM) model treats four-dimensional spacetime as an elastic membrane paired with a hypothetical mirror universe on its opposite side. Energy external to the membrane produces deformation and gravitational curvature analogous to General Relativity, whereas energy distributed uniformly within the membrane is treated as curvature-neutral.

Localised particle excitations are represented as oscillatory modes on one face of the membrane with corresponding mirror antiparticles on the opposite face. The model is not proposed as a replacement for established quantum field theory, but as a complementary deterministic framework in which wave interactions may reproduce quantum-like interference and entanglement analogues, potentially seeded by persistent sub-Planck-scale waves.

A modified elastic wave equation incorporating membrane tension, bending stiffness and spatially varying elastic properties is used to reproduce weak-field gravitational behaviour while simultaneously supporting stable standing-wave structures analogous to quantum interference.

Within this construction, photons are described as paired wave and anti-wave oscillations. The resulting states are intended to remain massless, support the expected polarisation structure and preserve U(1) gauge symmetry and Lorentz invariance.

The framework also considers whether time-averaged shifts in membrane stiffness can be related to the observed vacuum-energy scale and hence provide a possible interpretation of the cosmological constant.

The model remains speculative and requires substantially more theoretical development and observational testing to determine its quantitative relationship to established physics. It is presented as a geometric and deterministic perspective complementary to General Relativity and quantum field theory, with potential routes toward future theoretical investigation and analogue experiments.
