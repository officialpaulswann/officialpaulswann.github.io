Abstract

We present an artefact-driven simulation study of calibration-first deterministic standing-wave qubit control derived from the calibrated scalar sector of the Refined Space-Time Membrane (STM) model. Starting from the STM governing equation written in SI force-density form, coherence scales, gate-timing windows, stability margins and spectral constraints are derived directly from the calibrated elastic operator together with a single empirically extracted damping parameter.

A paired simulation pipeline combines full-field spectral integration of the STM partial differential equation with a reduced two-mode envelope model calibrated directly against the governing equation. Single- and two-qubit operations are assessed under common timing, slew, spectral-hygiene, stability and read-out acceptance windows.

Parametric operations are certified using exact Floquet analysis, demonstrating explicitly below-threshold stability. Robustness is assessed using multi-mode leakage tests and closed-loop noise injection, while reported numerical quantities are populated from machine-readable artefacts generated by the build pipeline.

The results establish a reproducible operating envelope for deterministic standing-wave qubit control in silico, conditional on the measured damping rate and carrier response of the embodiment under test. Within its declared scope, the study provides a falsifiable bridge between calibrated continuum dynamics and qubit-level control metrics.
